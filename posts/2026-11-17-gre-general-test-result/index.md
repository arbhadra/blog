I took the GRE General Test on 26 September 2024 and scored **321
(Verbal 154, Quantitative 167)** with **3.5 in Analytical Writing**. This post
explains what the test measures, why graduate programmes use it, and how this
result compares with the official ETS reference data, both for all test takers
and for applicants intending to study engineering.

## The GRE General Test

The GRE (Graduate Record Examinations) General Test is administered by ETS and
is accepted for admission by graduate and business schools worldwide. It
gives admissions committees a common, standardised measure for comparing
applicants from different universities, grading systems and countries.

Since September 2023, the test has taken about 1 hour 58 minutes and has three
measures:

| Measure | Format | Score scale |
|---|---|---|
| Verbal Reasoning | 2 sections, 27 questions | 130–170, 1-point steps |
| Quantitative Reasoning | 2 sections, 27 questions | 130–170, 1-point steps |
| Analytical Writing | 1 "Analyze an Issue" essay | 0–6, half-point steps |

Verbal and Quantitative Reasoning are adaptive by section: performance on the
first section sets the difficulty of the second. The Analytical Writing essay
is scored holistically by trained raters and by software. Scores remain
reportable for five years after the test date.

## Result

| Measure | Score | Percentile (score report) | Percentile (ETS data, 2022–25) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verbal Reasoning | 154 / 170 | 59th | 59th |
| Quantitative Reasoning | 167 / 170 | 78th | 75th |
| Analytical Writing | 3.5 / 6.0 | 41st | 40th |
| **Verbal + Quant** | **321 / 340** | — | — |

A percentile is the percentage of test takers who scored lower. The score
report shows the ranks in use when it was issued; the last column shows the
ranks in the latest ETS interpretive data, based on everyone who tested
between July 2022 and June 2025.

## Comparison with the reference groups

ETS reports the following means and standard deviations (SD) for all test
takers between July 2022 and June 2025 (about 790,000 people), and for
seniors and graduates who said they intended to study **engineering**
(about 49,000 people).

| Measure | My score | All test takers: mean (SD) | Difference | Engineering: mean (SD) | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Verbal | 154 | 151.39 (8.43) | +2.6 (+0.31 SD) | 151 (8) | +3 (+0.38 SD) |
| Quant | 167 | 157.62 (9.90) | +9.4 (+0.95 SD) | 161 (8) | +6 (+0.75 SD) |
| Writing | 3.5 | 3.46 (0.85) | +0.04 (+0.05 SD) | 3.4 (0.8) | +0.1 (+0.13 SD) |

In the engineering group, 38% of test takers scored 165 or higher in
Quantitative Reasoning, and 10.6% scored a perfect 170.

## Interpretation

- **Quantitative Reasoning is the strongest measure.** At 167, the score is
  almost one standard deviation above the overall mean, and 6 points above
  the mean of prospective engineering students. This is the measure that
  engineering and physical-science programmes weigh most heavily.
- **Verbal Reasoning is above average** for both reference groups. This
  matters for reading research literature and writing technical documents.
- **Analytical Writing is at the mean** for both groups. It is the measure
  with the most room for improvement. My later
  [IELTS](../2026-11-17-ielts-academic-result/) Writing band of 7.5 gives a second, independent measure of written English.

The quantitative result is consistent with my work in mechanical engineering
and computational mechanics, for example the
[FEM-2D finite element solver](../2026-11-15-fem2d-browser-solver/), which I
wrote and verified against analytical solutions.
